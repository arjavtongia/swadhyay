# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Jain households, mostly Digambar Jain families who read Hindi: children, parents and grandparents. Elders remain an important part of the audience; the app was first made for them and still defaults to large text. The app is now presented as "for everyone, with large text that also suits elders" (README).

They open it on a phone at home and in the temple: in the morning for darshan and pooja, reciting along with a path or stotra, checking whether today is ashtami or chaturdashi, reading a story to a child, or studying a granth slowly with its meaning. Hindi is the first language; English is there for people who read it more easily.

## Product Purpose

Swadhyay puts Jain scripture, worship and the Jain calendar in one free phone app that works offline. It holds 9 daily texts and guides, 170 poojas, paath and stotras, 54 granths, 33 illustrated stories, and a tithi calendar worked out on the phone. Success means someone recites or reads from it every day without help: they find the text, read it comfortably, and come back to where they stopped.

Four moments matter most (confirmed by the owner, all four equally):

1. Reciting path and pooja in the reader (Namokar, Bhaktamar, poojas), reading along or listening.
2. Checking today's tithi, ashtami and chaturdashi, festivals and the next 7 days.
3. Stories for the family: Jain kathas with pictures, and the story of the day.
4. Studying granths: long scriptures with meanings, chapters and go-to-number.

## Positioning

A devotional reader made with shraddha ("श्रद्धा सहित निर्मित"), not a content portal. It shows the original scripture in clear Devanagari with its meaning, gives drawn step-by-step guides for the temple and the pooja, reads aloud, and keeps the Jain calendar. All of it runs offline from one small static app whose texts anyone can correct as plain files on GitHub.

## Operating Context

- Phones first, often set down on the pooja chowki or held at arm's length while reciting; tablets and desktops sometimes.
- Used in daylight at home, in dim temple halls, and at night. The app opens in day colours; night colours, or following the phone's setting, can be chosen in Settings.
- Most texts are read one verse per screen with Previous and Next (swipes and arrow keys also work); short sutras are grouped on one screen. Poojas and short paath scroll as one page so they can be recited without tapping.
- "सुनें" (Listen) reads each verse aloud in a Hindi voice and turns the page by itself.
- Added to the phone's home screen as a PWA; works offline after the first open.
- Verses are shared to WhatsApp or copied.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Static site on GitHub Pages: plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, no build step, no framework, no server. Offline support through `sw.js`, whose `FILES` list and `VERSION` change when app files change.
- Content is plain text in `content/` in the format described in `content/README.md`; books, categories and pictures are listed in `books.json`, `categories.json` and `chitra.json`. Files in `content/pooja/` and `content/granth/` are regenerated by import scripts outside the repo.
- Interface in Hindi and English (`js/strings.js`); scripture text is never translated. Book names always show in Devanagari; numbers show in Latin digits.
- Settings: six text sizes (rem-based scaling of the whole app), day / night / same-as-phone colours, Roman script under each verse, listening speed, the opening bell on or off, a settings lock (press and hold 3 seconds to unlock).
- Bookmarks, continue reading, search in Hindi or English spellings and by voice, share or copy, go to a verse number, a story of the day and a sutra of the day.
- The tithi is calculated for sunrise in Delhi with purnimanta months; the app tells people to check their temple's panchang for fasts and festivals.
- Fonts: Noto Serif Devanagari, self-hosted (OFL). Icons: Tabler Icons (MIT). Drawings are authored SVG in `js/drawings.js`, `js/scenes.js` and `js/covers.js`.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: स्वाध्याय · Swadhyay. Greeting: जय जिनेन्द्र.
- The opening screen is a 3-second darshan of Bhagwan Mahavir, using a real photograph chosen by the owner: the murti of Shri Mahavirji (Wikimedia Commons, Dayodaya, CC BY-SA 3.0, credited in the app) seen through the app's cusped brass arch on a deep maroon-brown ground. The name स्वाध्याय is written in, with Swadhyay beneath a brass rule, and the credit "श्रद्धा सहित निर्मित · by Arjav" sits as one quiet line at the foot. Its closing light spreads from the bhamandal into the app's own page colour. A single soft temple bell sounds as it opens, where the phone allows sound without a tap; Settings can turn it off for the temple or quiet places. The earlier drawn murti was retired at the owner's request because it looked unprofessional.
- Digambar Jain tradition: Digambar recensions of the texts and Digambar iconography (the Jina unadorned, seated or standing).
- Sources, translators and photographers are credited in the app.

## Product Principles

1. The text comes first. Scripture is the reason to open the app; every screen should make it easier to read, recite and return to.
2. Nobody should need help. One obvious next step on every screen, plain words in the reader's language, nothing that works only through a hidden gesture.
3. Made with devotion. Respectful of the tradition and calm; never commercial, never gamified.
4. Works anywhere. Offline, on old phones, at every text size.
5. Open to correction. Texts stay plain files that anyone can fix.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Large readable text by default, with six text sizes; every screen has to hold up at the largest one.
- Big tap targets (at least 48px) and labelled controls: elders with low vision or unsteady hands are part of the audience.
- High contrast in both day and night colours; reduced motion is respected.
- Hindi first, with English. Devanagari must render correctly: conjuncts, matras and the shirorekha.
